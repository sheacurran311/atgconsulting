# ATG intake and first-call readiness audit

## What the public form is for

Give Shea enough information to prepare a useful 20-minute conversation: who the prospect is, what they want to achieve, which goal comes first, one concrete example, how they describe success, and any known timing or budget. Use the call to establish feasibility and scope. The approved proposal remains the commercial record.

## Findings and changes

| Area reviewed | Finding | Implemented change or outstanding check |
|---|---|---|
| Start and review screens | Copy promised an audit and an estimated completion time without measured completion data. | Explain the meeting purpose and show the upper question count. |
| Public question sequence | The detailed tree could reach 29 questions, including AI experience, operational baselines and permissions before qualification. | Use 8 to 10 questions including contact details. Company and first priority depend on audience and number of goals. |
| Service coverage | Logo and identity requests inherited website migration questions. Custom and mixed goals needed distinct preparation. | Use service-specific prompts and keep secondary goals in the private brief. Unknown business requests use open discovery. |
| Resume, imported answers and edits | Existing sessions can contain useful detailed answers from the previous tree. | Preserve applicable answers and display them in preparation. Changing the primary service invalidates context collected under the previous service. |
| First-call worksheet | The backend computed missing information, but the worksheet exposed only a collapsed agenda and blank fields. | Show submitted context, open meeting decisions, and proposal and kickoff prompts directly in Admin Discovery. Saved call answers remove covered topics from the meeting queue. |
| Approval and delivery | Prospect statements and reviewed fit-call outcomes serve different purposes. | Keep original answers as reference. The consultant records the confirmed brief, first deliverable, owner and acceptance criterion in the existing revision-controlled worksheet. |
| Validation boundaries | An automated path test cannot measure abandonment or confirm the signed-in experience. | Test a new submission, resume, edit, imported questionnaire and consultant save/reload in Replit. Completion time and conversion remain unmeasured. |

## Question waterfall

| Stage | What Shea needs by the end | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Before the call | Priority, example, success, timing, budget if known, and secondary requests | Submitted intake and first-call playbook |
| During the call | Actual situation, decision-maker, constraints, feasibility, first deliverable, success criterion and next action | Private fit-call worksheet |
| Before quoting | Deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities, revisions, costs, milestone schedule and approval terms appropriate to each requested service | Reviewed proposal |
| After acceptance | Assets, account owners, access permissions, test evidence and handoff responsibilities for the accepted scope | Delivery handoff |

The proposal and kickoff prompts are preparation guides. They do not create paid entitlements, assign tasks or establish that evidence has been collected.

## Example

A prospect requests a logo and a website, putting the logo first. Shea sees the intended audience and usage alongside the website backlog. On the call, identify the design approver and launch date. Before quoting, settle concept rounds, final file formats, pages and who supplies copy. At kickoff, collect existing artwork and confirm domain ownership.
